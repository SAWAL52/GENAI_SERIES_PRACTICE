THE SOLAR SYSTEM
================

1. Overview
-----------
The Solar System consists of the Sun and all the objects that travel around it.
These objects include eight planets, their moons, dwarf planets, asteroids,
comets, and smaller pieces of rock and ice.

The Sun is a star at the center of the Solar System. Its gravity keeps the
planets and many other objects in orbit. The Sun also provides most of the
light and heat that make Earth suitable for life.

2. The Eight Planets
--------------------
The eight planets, in order from the Sun, are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars,
Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and the smallest of the eight planets.
It has a rocky surface and almost no atmosphere.

Venus is the second planet from the Sun. It has a very thick atmosphere,
mostly made of carbon dioxide. Its atmosphere traps heat, making Venus the
hottest planet in the Solar System.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun. It has liquid water on its surface
and is the only planet currently known to support life.

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun. It is often called the Red Planet
because iron minerals in its soil give the surface a reddish appearance.
Scientists study Mars to learn whether life may have existed there in the past.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System. It is a gas giant with a
large storm called the Great Red Spot. Jupiter has many moons.

Saturn is famous for its large and bright ring system. It is a gas giant,
and its rings are made mostly of pieces of ice and rock.

Uranus is an ice giant. It rotates on its side compared with most other
planets, giving it an unusual pattern of seasons.

Neptune is the eighth and most distant planet from the Sun. It is an ice
giant known for very strong winds and a cold atmosphere.

3. Other Objects in the Solar System
------------------------------------
Dwarf planets are round objects that orbit the Sun but have not cleared
their orbital paths of other objects. Pluto is a well-known dwarf planet.

Asteroids are rocky objects that orbit the Sun. Many are found in the
asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

Comets are made mainly of ice, dust, and rocky material. When a comet
approaches the Sun, some of its ice turns into gas and can form a glowing
coma and a tail.

Moons are natural objects that orbit planets or dwarf planets. Earth's Moon
is the only natural satellite of Earth.

4. Earth's Rotation and Revolution
----------------------------------
Earth rotates around an imaginary line called its axis. One complete rotation
takes about 24 hours and causes day and night.

Earth also revolves around the Sun. One complete revolution takes about
365.25 days. This movement defines a year. The extra quarter of a day is
accounted for by adding a leap day to most calendar years that are divisible
by four, with additional century-year rules.

Earth's axis is tilted. As Earth travels around the Sun, this tilt causes
the seasons. Seasons are not mainly caused by changes in Earth's distance
from the Sun.

5. Why Study the Solar System?
------------------------------
Studying the Solar System helps scientists understand how planets and other
celestial objects form and change over time. Space missions use spacecraft,
orbiters, landers, and rovers to collect information.

Research about other planets also helps scientists compare their environments
with Earth. This can improve our understanding of Earth's climate, geology,
and place in space.

6. Quick Facts
--------------
- The Sun is a star, not a planet.
- There are eight recognized planets in the Solar System.
- Earth is the third planet from the Sun.
- Jupiter is the largest planet.
- Mercury is the smallest planet.
- Saturn has a prominent ring system.
- The asteroid belt lies mainly between Mars and Jupiter.
- Pluto is classified as a dwarf planet.
- Earth's rotation causes day and night.
- Earth's axial tilt is the main reason for the seasons.

7. Common Questions
-------------------
Q: What is the Solar System?
A: It is the Sun and the planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets,
   and other objects held in orbit by gravity.

Q: Which planet is closest to the Sun?
A: Mercury.

Q: Which planet is the largest?
A: Jupiter.

Q: Why does Earth have seasons?
A: Earth's axis is tilted as the planet revolves around the Sun.

Q: Is Pluto a planet?
A: Pluto is classified as a dwarf planet.

Q: What causes day and night?
A: Earth's rotation on its axis.
